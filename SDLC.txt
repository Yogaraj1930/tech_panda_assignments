What is SDLC?
    SDLC stands for software developement life cycle, it is a step by step process that team follows to plan, build, test and maintain software.
    It is used to maintain better quality, lower cost, predictable timelines and better team work.
    It has seven phases. Planning, Requirement, Design, Developement, Testing, Deployment and Maintainance.
    Planning - Design what to build and whether it's worth building. Set goal, scope, budget and timeline. Persons involved - senior engineer, business leaders, project managers and client.
    Requirement - Understand exactly what users and business need. Will right down functional and nonfunctional needs. Persons involved - Business analyst, product owners, clients and end users.
    Design - Create the blueprint before writing a single line of code. Design the architecture, Sketch the screens, Plan the database. Persons involved - Software architects, UI/UX designers, senior developers.
    Development -  The blueprint becomes real, working code. Developers write code following the design documents, Work is split into small tasks across the team. Persons involved - Front-end, back-end and full-stack developers.
    Testing - Hunt down bugs before your users ever meet them. Verify the software matches the requirements, Run unit, integration, system and acceptance tests. Persons involved - QA engineers, testers, developers, sometimes real users
    Deployment - Ship it! The software goes live to real users. Release the software to the production environment, Often rolled out gradually. Persons involved - DevOps engineers, release managers, support teams
    Maintenance - Keep it healthy, secure and improving — for years. Fix bugs that users discover in the real world, Release updates, patches and security fixes. Persons involved - Support engineers, developers, product manager.
