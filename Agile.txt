What is Agile methodology?
    Agile methodology is a way of managing and developing projects—especially software projects—where you build the product in small steps, get feedback frequently, and adapt to changes.
Instead of deciding everything at the beginning and delivering the entire product at the end, Agile works in short cycles called iterations or sprints.

Agile emphasizes:

People and collaboration — developers, testers, designers, customers, and stakeholders communicate frequently.
Working software — prioritize something that actually works over producing lots of documentation.
Customer feedback — involve the customer throughout development rather than only at the end.
Responding to change — requirements can change, and the team adapts.
Continuous improvement — after each iteration, the team looks at what went well and what should improve.

What is the Waterfall Method?

The Waterfall methodology is a traditional project management and software development approach where work happens step by step in a fixed sequence.
Think of it like a waterfall: once you move down to the next stage, you generally don't go back.

Stages:

Requirements
     ↓
Design
     ↓
Development
     ↓
Testing
     ↓
Deployment
     ↓
Maintenance
